      The Crooked Lantern

      Last Call

      Four chairs, twenty cards and a revolver with a single round in it. Bring friends, or sit with the regulars.

      Lighting the lamps

      How to play

      1. Each round names a table card: King, Queen or Ace. Everyone is dealt 5 from a deck of 6 Kings, 6 Queens, 6 Aces and 2 Jokers.
      2. On your turn, lay 1 to 3 cards face down and claim they all match the table card. Jokers always match. Nobody checks unless someone calls.
      3. The next player either plays on or calls Liar. The cards are flipped.
      4. Whoever was wrong pulls the trigger. Each player's revolver hides one round in six chambers, so every pull you survive makes the next one worse.
      5. If everyone else has emptied their hand, you must call the last play.
      6. With Devil rules the deck holds one Devil, played alone. If a call turns it over, everyone but its player pulls the trigger.
      7. Watch their eyes. Some of them flicker when they're nervous. Every turn has a clock; when it runs out, the house plays for you.
      1. Everyone shakes five dice under a cup and peeks at their own. Ones are wild: they count as any face.
      2. On your turn, bid how many dice on the whole table show a face, like “seven fours”. Each bid must raise the last: more dice, or as many dice of a higher face.
      3. Or call Liar on the last bid. Every cup lifts and the dice are counted. Too few and the bidder loses; enough and the caller does.
      4. The loser drinks from their own tray of six glasses. One is poisoned, and the tray is never refilled, so every glass you survive makes the next one worse.
      5. With Traditional rules you may call Spot on instead: exactly right and everyone else drinks; wrong and you do.

      1. Everyone starts with the same eleven money cards: 1 to 25 crowns, 106 in all. Money you spend is gone for good.
      2. One status card is turned at a time: ten curios worth 1 to 10, three Patron's Favours that double your status, and three disgraces.
      3. Bid in turn by adding money to the cards in front of you, beating the high bid, or pass to take yours back. The last bidder pays and takes the card.
      4. A disgrace runs in reverse: the first to pass takes it and keeps their money; everyone else pays. Spilled Ale costs a curio, Bad Debt is minus 5, Public Scandal halves you.
      5. When the fourth gilded card turns (favours and the scandal), the evening ends. The poorest player is cast out; the richest in status wins.
